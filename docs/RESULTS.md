## 1. Setup

- **Test week:** 7.0 simulated days with **84 injected attack stages** (labeled ground truth, hidden from the detectors): the standalone scenarios S1-S9 plus the stages of 6 chained kill chains (S1→S2→S3→S4→S5; each stage counts separately).
- **False-positive week:** a separate benign-only week (7.0 days, same identities, independent event stream). False positives per day are measured here, so they are not diluted by attacks.
- **Training:** benign-only data from the same synthetic organisation (10 days: the first 5 build per-identity baselines, the last 5 give training windows). The anomaly threshold is calibrated on the benign week to about 6 flagged windows/day.
- **Definitions.** *Incident recall* = share of injected attacks overlapped by at least one incident. *Event-level* precision/recall = over individual CloudTrail events placed in alerts. *Time to detect* = simulated seconds from the attack's first malicious event to the first alert that covers it (anomaly alerts are raised when their 15-minute window completes, so they lag rules).

## 12. Caveats: read before quoting any number

- **Synthetic data.** Benign traffic and attacks come from the same generator family, so metrics are optimistic compared with a real account. Real-data checks (flaws.cloud, Stratus Red Team samples) are reported separately in `docs/REAL_DATA_FINDINGS.md`.
- **Rules were written knowing the scenarios.** High rule recall on S1-S8 is expected; the robustness table above is the fairer view of what rules can do against a careful attacker.
- **Stealth is a model, not reality.** The levels remove specific signals (user agent, region/country, hours, pace). Real adversaries vary in other ways.
- **Small samples.** Each scenario has a handful of injections per week, so per-scenario recall moves in large steps (one missed attack is a double-digit percentage swing).
- **FP rates depend on the calibrated threshold** (a target number of flagged benign windows per day) and on the synthetic noise models (on-call nights, a traveling developer, onboarding, maintenance). Real organisations are noisier.
- **Labels never reach the detectors** (enforced by tests), but the thresholds were tuned while looking at these same attack types, so some optimism remains.
